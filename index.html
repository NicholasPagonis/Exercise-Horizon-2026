Participant information and downloads

Getting there

Park at Belmont Sports & Recreation Club, 400 Abernethy Rd, Cloverdale WA 6105, then walk across the road to Forster Park Community Centre. The route is marked on the map below.

Roles

Volunteers take one of three roles, differing in start time, where you go and which cards you carry. Your role is allocated by the Emergency Operations team and sent to you closer to the exercise date.

Patient

You play a simulated casualty at the centre of the scenario. Report to the check-in point at Forster Park for moulage (injury makeup) — start times are staggered by how much your injuries need, heaviest first. You then move out to the airfield incident site, where St John WA, ARFFS, DFES and other responders triage and treat you as they would in a real emergency, ending either at a notional hospital or cleared on scene.

Cards: patient card only, with your details and injury information. Linked family member: none.

Patient and Passenger

A combined role: treated in the field, then reunified with a family member. You start at Forster Park with the other patients, take your moulage and move out to the airfield as a casualty. Your injuries are deliberately non-critical (a Priority 3), so once you are cleared you switch to your passenger identity, board a bus and return to the Survivor Reception to meet your allocated family member or friend.

Cards: your patient card, plus a green passenger (PAX) card holding your identity and reunification details. Linked family member: one.

Family / Friend

You represent the loved ones arriving at the airport for news of someone on the aircraft, which is what puts the reunification process to the test. You do not go to the airfield. Arrive later in the day — around 11am to midday — and report to Forster Park for a brief from the Volunteer Coordinators, then wait at the Survivor Reception for your matched passenger to be discharged and transported back.

Cards: a single red family/friend card with the details you need to complete the match. Linked passenger: one.

Reunification

Reunification brings the two halves of the exercise back together at the Survivor Reception at Forster Park: cleared passengers are bussed back from the incident site to the family and friends waiting there since late morning.

Each Patient and Passenger is matched to exactly one Family / Friend, and the pair complete the match using their cards — the green PAX card carries the passenger’s identity and reunification details, the red card what the family member needs to confirm it. Because the pairing is one-to-one, both halves need to attend, so tell the Emergency Operations team early if your availability changes.

What to bring

Bring with you

  • Enclosed footwear
  • A reusable water bottle
  • Older clothing you don’t mind getting dirty (casualty roles only)
  • Any personal medication you may need

Provided on the day

  • Drawstring bag and participant lanyard
  • A pre-exercise briefing on arrival
  • Lunch — burger (or wrap), hot chips and a drink
  • On-site first aid support

Important information

  1. Participation in the exercise results in automatic consent to being photographed and videographed for the duration of the activity. If you do not wish to be recorded, notify the Emergency Operations team to withdraw your participation.
  2. Perth Airport employees are responsible for confirming their attendance with their line manager before the exercise. Please ensure you have approval to be released from your usual duties on the day.
  3. Your attendance is confirmed, however your specific role, reporting time and location have not yet been assigned. These details are allocated at the discretion of the Emergency Operations team and will be sent to you in a separate email closer to the exercise date.
