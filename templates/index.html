Selection Process

Every volunteer starts by submitting an expression of interest. From there the Emergency Operations team either confirms your attendance and assigns your details closer to the day, or places you on the reserve list if the exercise is already at capacity.

Getting there

Report to {{OBS_LOCATION}}. Follow the signs inside the Alpha Building to the Airport Experience Centre on the ground floor.

Drive in past the roundabout to the Alpha Building access gate and use gate code {{OBS_GATE_CODE}} to enter the staff car park. Park in the {{OBS_PARKING}}, then walk the short route to the building entrance. Your vehicle stays there all day and you return to it at the end.

Bus transport is provided for the whole circuit, so you do not drive between the viewing area and Forster Park.

How the day runs

08:00 Walk in to the Alpha Building, follow the signs to the AEC and sign in. Registration, safety briefing, issue of hi-vis and conduct expectations.
08:30 Board the bus and travel to the viewing area, in position before the exercise commences.
09:00 Observe the multi-agency response from Emergency Gate 6 and other areas of the airfield.
11:30 Move by bus to Forster Park to observe the reunification process.
12:30 Lunch, provided at Forster Park by the Salvation Army.
13:15 Return by bus to the AEC, and are released to depart in your own vehicle.

You remain escorted and within the monitored viewing area at all times. Follow the directions of exercise staff and airport authorised officers throughout the day.

Roles

Volunteers take one of three roles, differing in start time, where you go and which cards you carry. Your role is allocated by the Emergency Operations team and sent to you closer to the exercise date. Open a role below to read what the day looks like in it.

Patient

You play a simulated casualty at the centre of the scenario. Report to the check-in point at Forster Park for moulage (injury makeup) — arrival times are staggered by how much your injuries need, heaviest first, so come at the time you are given rather than earlier.

You then move out to the airfield incident site, where St John WA, ARFFS, DFES and other responders triage and treat you as they would in a real emergency. Your morning ends either at a notional hospital or cleared on scene.

Cards: patient card only, with your details and injury information. Linked family member: none.

How to read your card

An example card, not anybody’s real allocation. Yours folds down the middle, and both halves carry your card number, role and group, so it reads the same whichever way you pick it up. Open it in a new tab if it does not load here.

  • Card number, role and group. Printed on both halves. Your group is your arrival time — quote the card number if you need to ask us anything.
  • Personal details. The identity you are playing. Give these if a responder asks who you are.
  • Presentation. How to behave, where you are found and what is visible. This is the part worth learning before the day.
  • First look. Airway, breathing rate, capillary refill and pulse — the quick observations a responder takes on first contact.
  • Your numbers on reassessment. The fuller set taken when a clinician comes back to you. For the responders to read, not for you to recite.
  • What a clinician finds. The injuries they will discover on examination. Your moulage is made up to match this.
  • If you are not treated. Your cue. If nobody has treated you within ten minutes, change your behaviour the way this box describes.
  • NO DUFF. The stop phrase for a genuine emergency. Say “NO DUFF” loudly, stop acting, and show this side of the card to the nearest Safety Advisor or Exercise Controller.

A patient card carries no reunification block. Nobody is waiting for you at the Survivor Reception, so there is no linked family member on it.

Reunification

You are not part of the reunification process. Without a linked passenger identity there is nobody waiting for you at the Survivor Reception, so your day finishes at the incident site.

Patient and Passenger

A combined role: treated in the field, then reunified with a family member. You start at Forster Park with the other patients, take your moulage and move out to the airfield as a casualty. Your injuries are deliberately non-critical (a Priority 3), so once you are cleared you switch to your passenger identity, board a bus and return to the Survivor Reception to meet your allocated family member or friend.

Cards: your patient card, plus a passenger (PAX) card holding your identity and reunification details. Linked family member: one.

Reunification

Reunification brings the two halves of the exercise back together at the Survivor Reception at Forster Park. Once you are cleared at the incident site you are bussed back to the family and friends who have been waiting there since late morning.

You are matched to exactly one Family / Friend, and the two of you complete the match using your cards — your PAX card carries your identity and reunification details, their red card what they need to confirm it. Because the pairing is one-to-one, both halves need to attend, so tell the Emergency Operations team early if your availability changes.

Family / Friend

You represent the loved ones arriving at the airport for news of someone on the aircraft, which is what puts the reunification process to the test. You do not go to the airfield. Arrive at 11:00 and report to Forster Park for a brief from the Volunteer Coordinators, then wait at the Survivor Reception for your matched passenger to be discharged and transported back.

Cards: a single red family/friend card with the details you need to complete the match. Linked passenger: one.

How to read your card

An example card, not anybody’s real allocation. Family and friend cards are red, so they are easy to tell apart from the passenger cards at the Survivor Reception. Open it in a new tab if it does not load here.

  • Card number and role. Family and friend card numbers start with F. Quote it if you need to ask us anything.
  • Personal details. The identity you are playing for the day — your name, age and date of birth.
  • Who you are looking for. The passenger you are matched with: their name, card number, age, date of birth and flight number. This is what you give the reunification team, and what confirms the match when they are brought back.
  • NO DUFF. The stop phrase for a genuine emergency. Say “NO DUFF” loudly, stop acting, and show this side of the card to the nearest Safety Advisor or Exercise Controller.

There are no clinical observations on a family or friend card. You are not treated and you do not go to the airfield, so it carries identity and matching details only.

Reunification

Reunification brings the two halves of the exercise back together at the Survivor Reception at Forster Park. Cleared passengers are bussed back from the incident site to the family and friends waiting there since late morning.

You are matched to exactly one Patient and Passenger, and the two of you complete the match using your cards — their PAX card carries the passenger’s identity and reunification details, your red card what you need to confirm it. Because the pairing is one-to-one, both halves need to attend, so tell the Emergency Operations team early if your availability changes.

Arrival times

Casualty roles are called in three moulage groups, staggered by how much injury makeup the role needs. Family and friends come later, for reunification only. The Emergency Operations team tells you which group you are in — take the calendar entry that matches, and it will replace the hold-the-date one.

Important information

  1. Participation in the exercise results in automatic consent to being photographed and videographed for the duration of the activity. If you do not wish to be recorded, notify the Emergency Operations team to withdraw your participation.
  2. Perth Airport employees are responsible for confirming their attendance with their line manager before the exercise. Please ensure you have approval to be released from your usual duties on the day.
  3. Your attendance is confirmed, however your specific role, reporting time and location have not yet been assigned. These details are allocated at the discretion of the Emergency Operations team and will be sent to you in a separate email closer to the exercise date.
  4. You remain escorted and within the monitored viewing area for the whole day, and the bus circuit runs to a fixed schedule. There is no option to move between locations independently, so please stay with your group and follow the directions of exercise staff throughout.
